Exploring the concluding invocations, the golden vessel hiding the face of Truth, and final prayers for divine grace and guidance.

Mantras & Detailed Breakdown

Study the Sanskrit verses, transliteration, and closing invocations of the Upanishad.

Mantra 15 Verse 15 of 18

हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण सत्यस्यापिहितं मुखम् |
तत्त्वं पूषन्नपावृणु सत्यधर्माय दृष्टये || १५ ||

Hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasyāpihitaṃ mukham |
Tattvaṃ pūṣannapāvṛṇu satyadharmāya dṛṣṭaye || 15 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

hiraṇmayena pātreṇa: by a golden vessel / covering
satyasya mukham: the face of Truth
apihitam: is covered
tat tvam pūṣan: that, you (remove), O Pūṣan
apāvṛṇu: uncover, remove
satyadharmāya: for one devoted to Truth / the law of Truth
dṛṣṭaye: for seeing

English Translation

“The face of Truth is covered by a golden vessel. Uncover it, O Pūṣan, for me, devoted to Truth, so that I may see.”

Philosophical Commentary

The seeker asks for the radiant covering to be lifted so that Truth may be directly seen. The golden vessel is often read as a symbol of a brilliant veil between appearance and ultimate reality; the prayer asks not for more appearances, but for vision beyond them.

Mantras 16 & 17 Verses 16–17 of 18

पूषन्नेकर्षे यम सूर्य प्राजापत्य व्यूह रश्मीन् समूह |
तेजो यत्ते रूपं कल्याणतमं तत्ते पश्यामि योऽसावसौ पुरुषः सोऽहमस्मि || १६ ||

Pūṣannekarṣe yama sūrya prājāpatya vyūha raśmīn samūha |
Tejo yatte rūpaṃ kalyāṇatamaṃ tatte paśyāmi yo'sāvasau puruṣaḥ so'hamasmi || 16 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 16

pūṣan: O nourisher
ekaṛṣe: O sole seer
yama: O controller
sūrya: O Sun
prājāpatya: O child of Prajāpati
vyūha raśmīn: draw apart your rays
samūha tejaḥ: gather / withdraw your radiance
yat te rūpam kalyāṇatamam: your most auspicious form
tat te paśyāmi: may I see that of yours
yaḥ asau asau puruṣaḥ: that Person who is there
saḥ aham asmi: I am that

English Translation — Mantra 16

“O Pūṣan, sole seer, controller, Sun, child of Prajāpati, draw apart your rays and gather your radiance, so that I may see your most auspicious form. The Person who is there—that am I.”

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 16

The prayer moves from the visible radiance of the Sun toward the auspicious Person beyond it. The closing “I am that” expresses an intimate identity or recognition of the Self with that Person in the text’s contemplative vision; it is not merely a description of physical sameness.

वायुरनिलममृतमथेदं भस्मान्तं शरीरम् ।
ॐ क्रतो स्मर कृतं स्मर क्रतो स्मर कृतं स्मर ॥ १७ ॥

Vāyur anilam amṛtam atha idaṃ bhasmāntaṃ śarīram |
Oṃ krato smara kṛtaṃ smara krato smara kṛtaṃ smara || 17 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 17

vāyuḥ: the life-breath / wind
anilam amṛtam: the immortal, all-pervading air
atha: then, and
idam śarīram: this body
bhasmāntam: ending in ashes
oṃ: sacred syllable
krato: O mind / will (vocative)
smara: remember
kṛtam: what has been done, the deed

English Translation — Mantra 17

“May the life-breath merge with the immortal air; then let this body end in ashes. O mind, remember what has been done; remember, O mind, what has been done.”

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 17

This departure prayer acknowledges bodily mortality while asking for the life-breath to return to the immortal principle. The repeated call to remember one's deeds may be read as a final appeal to recollection and moral accountability at the threshold of death.

Mantra 18 Verse 18 of 18

अग्ने नय सुपथा राये अस्मान् विश्वानि देव वयुनानि विद्वान् ।
युयोध्यस्मज्जुहुराणमेनो भूयिष्ठां ते नमौक्तिं विधेम ॥ १८ ॥

Agne naya supathā rāye asmān viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān |
Yuyodhy asmaj juhurāṇam eno bhūyiṣṭhāṃ te namauktiṃ vidhema || 18 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

agne: O Agni
naya: lead
supathā: by the good path
rāye: to well-being / good fortune
asmān: us
viśvāni vayunāni: all paths, ways, or deeds
deva vidvān: O divine one, knower
yuyodhi: remove, keep away
asmat juhurāṇam enaḥ: from us the crooked / harmful sin
bhūyiṣṭhāṃ te namauktim: our highest expression of homage to you
vidhema: we offer / perform

English Translation

“O Agni, knower of all ways, lead us by the good path to well-being. Keep from us the crooked sin. To you we offer our highest words of homage.”

Philosophical Commentary

The Upanishad concludes with a humble prayer for guidance, discernment, and release from wrongdoing. Agni is invoked as the divine guide who knows the paths; the closing act is an offering of reverence rather than a claim that the seeker is already beyond the need for grace.

Sanskrit text checked against SanskritDocuments.org: Īśopaniṣat. Translations and interpretations are provided here for study.

Core Philosophical Takeaway

Module 4 concludes the Ishavasya Upanishad with personal invocations. The seeker addresses cosmic forces such as Sūrya and Agni, asking for the veil over Truth to be lifted and for guidance on a good path. The identity expressed in Mantra 16 is understood differently across philosophical traditions; nondual readings emphasize realization of the Self’s identity with the supreme Person.
