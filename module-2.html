Module 2 • The Nature of the Atman

Mantras 4 to 8 Breakdown

Exploring the profound paradoxes of the unmoving yet swift Atman, and how realizing universal unity eradicates all sorrow, grief, and delusion.

Mantras & Detailed Breakdown

Each mantra includes the Sanskrit text, transliteration, word-by-word meaning, English translation, and philosophical commentary.

Mantra 4 Verse 4 of 18

अनेजदेकं मनसो जवीयो नैनद्देवा आप्नुवन्पूर्वमर्षत् ।
तद्धावतोऽन्यानत्येति तिष्ठत्तस्मिन्नपो मातरिश्वा दधाति ॥ ४ ॥

Anejad ekaṃ manaso javīyo nainad devā āpnuvan pūrvam arṣat |
Tad dhāvato 'nyān atyeti tiṣṭhat tasminn apo mātariśvā dadhāti || 4 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

anejat: unmoving
ekam: one, the One
manasaḥ javīyaḥ: swifter than the mind
enat devāḥ na āpnuvan: the senses do not reach it
pūrvam arṣat: it has gone before them
tat dhāvataḥ anyān atyeti: it surpasses others who run
tiṣṭhat: while remaining still
tasmin: in it
mātariśvā apaḥ dadhāti: Mātariśvan (the cosmic life-breath) sustains the waters and activity

English Translation

“The One is unmoving, swifter than the mind. The senses cannot reach it, for it has gone before them. Though standing still, it outstrips those who run. In it, Mātariśvan sustains the waters.”

Philosophical Commentary

The Self is not an object moving through space; it is the unchanging reality presupposed by every movement of mind and senses. The senses cannot grasp it as an external object because it is the very ground of their activity. The image of Mātariśvan sustaining the cosmic powers points to the order and life supported within that reality.

Mantra 5 Verse 5 of 18

तदेजति तन्नैजति तद्दूरे तद्वन्तिके ।
तदन्तरस्य सर्वस्य तदु सर्वस्यास्य बाह्यतः ॥ ५ ॥

Tad ejati tan na ejati tad dūre tad u antike |
Tad antar asya sarvasya tad u sarvasya asya bāhyataḥ || 5 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

tat ejati: that moves
tat na ejati: that does not move
tat dūre: that is far
tat u antike: that indeed is near
tat antaḥ asya sarvasya: that is within all this
tat u sarvasya asya bāhyataḥ: that is also outside all this

English Translation

“It moves, and it does not move. It is far away, and it is near. It is within all this, and it is also outside all this.”

Philosophical Commentary

The verse uses paired opposites to show that the Self cannot be confined by ordinary ideas of motion or distance. It is “near” as the innermost reality of experience and “far” from the grasp of objectifying thought; it is within every being while not limited to any one being.

Mantra 6 Verse 6 of 18

यस्तु सर्वाणि भूतान्यात्मन्येवानुपश्यति ।
सर्वभूतेषु चात्मानं ततो न विजुगुप्सते ॥ ६ ॥

Yas tu sarvāṇi bhūtāny ātmany evānupaśyati |
Sarvabhūteṣu cātmānaṃ tato na vijugupsate || 6 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

yaḥ tu: but whoever
sarvāṇi bhūtāni: all beings
ātmani eva anupaśyati: sees distinctly in the Self
sarvabhūteṣu ca: and in all beings
ātmānam: the Self
tataḥ: then, from that vision
na vijugupsate: does not recoil from or despise (any being)

English Translation

“Whoever sees all beings in the Self alone, and the Self in all beings, does not recoil from or despise anyone.”

Philosophical Commentary

This verse describes the ethical and experiential consequence of insight: when the same Self is recognized in every being, contempt and alienation lose their foundation. The vision is reciprocal—beings are seen in the Self, and the Self is recognized in beings—rather than a claim that the separate body or personality is identical to everything else.

Mantra 7 Verse 7 of 18

यस्मिन्सर्वाणि भूतान्यात्मैवाभूद्विजानतः ।
तत्र को मोहः कः शोक एकत्वमनुपश्यतः ॥ ७ ॥

Yasmin sarvāṇi bhūtāny ātmaivābhūd vijānataḥ |
Tatra ko mohaḥ kaḥ śoka ekatvam anupaśyataḥ || 7 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

yasmin: when, or in whom
sarvāṇi bhūtāni: all beings
ātma eva abhūt: have become the Self alone
vijānataḥ: for one who knows
tatra: then, in that vision
kaḥ mohaḥ: what delusion?
kaḥ śokaḥ: what grief?
ekatvam anupaśyataḥ: for one who sees oneness

English Translation

“For the one who knows, in whom all beings have become the very Self, what delusion or grief can there be for one who sees oneness?”

Philosophical Commentary

Mantra 7 deepens the vision of Mantra 6. Seeing unity removes the fundamental misapprehension that gives rise to grief over an ultimately separate self and world. This is a statement about the freedom of clear knowledge, not a denial that people experience ordinary loss or pain.

Mantra 8 Verse 8 of 18

स पर्यगाच्छुक्रमकायमव्रणमस्नाविरं शुद्धमपापविद्धम् ।
कविर्मनीषी परिभूः स्वयम्भूर्याथातथ्यतोऽर्थान् व्यदधाच्छाश्वतीभ्यः समाभ्यः ॥ ८ ॥

Sa paryagāc chukram akāyam avraṇam asnāviraṃ śuddham apāpaviddham |
Kavir manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūr yāthātathyato 'rthān vyadadhāc chāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ || 8 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

saḥ paryagāt: that One has pervaded all
śukram: radiant, pure
akāyam: bodiless
avraṇam: without wound or blemish
asnāviraṃ: without sinews
śuddham: pure
apāpaviddham: untouched by evil
kaviḥ: seer
manīṣī: wise, discerning
paribhūḥ: transcendent, all-surpassing
svayambhūḥ: self-existent
yāthātathyataḥ arthān vyadadhāt: ordered things according to their nature
śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ: for all time, through everlasting years

English Translation

“That One has pervaded all: radiant, bodiless, unblemished, sinewless, pure, untouched by evil; the seer, wise, all-surpassing, self-existent. From everlasting, it has ordered all things according to their nature.”

Philosophical Commentary

This verse gathers the preceding teaching into a description of the Self as unembodied, pure, and self-existent—not a limited physical entity or an agent subject to ordinary defects. Its wisdom and sovereignty are expressed through the ordering of things according to their nature. The verse thus connects the Self’s transcendence with the intelligible order of the world.

Core Philosophical Takeaway

Mantras 4–5 describe the Self through paradoxes: unmoving yet swifter than the mind, both near and far, within and beyond all beings. Mantras 6–7 show how recognizing the Self in all beings dissolves alienation, delusion, and grief. Mantra 8 describes that reality as pure, bodiless, self-existent, and the ground of the ordered world.
