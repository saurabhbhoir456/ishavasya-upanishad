Module 1 • Life in the World & Divine Pervasiveness

Mantras 1 to 3 Breakdown

Examining the foundational principles of divine pervasiveness, purposeful worldly action through non-attachment, and the peril of spiritual ignorance.

1 Mantra 1: Divine Pervasiveness & Detachment

Verse 1 of 18

ईशावास्यमिदं सर्वं यत्किञ्च जगत्यां जगत् |

तेन त्यक्तेन भुञ्जीथा मा गृधः कस्य स्विद्धनम् || १ ||

īśāvāsyamidaṃ sarvaṃ yatkiñca jagatyāṃ jagat |
tena tyaktena bhuñjīthā mā gṛdhaḥ kasya sviddhanam || 1 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

Īśā avāsyam: Pervaded by the Lord
idam sarvam: All this (universe)
yat kiñca: Whatever
jagatyām jagat: Moving in this changing world
tena tyaktena: Through that renunciation (detachment)
bhuñjīthāḥ: Protect/enjoy yourself
mā gṛdhaḥ: Do not covet
kasya svid dhanam: Whose is wealth? (Whose property is it anyway?)

English Translation

"All this—whatever moves or is changeful in this universe—is pervaded by the Supreme Lord. Protect yourself and enjoy through the spirit of detachment; do not covet anyone’s wealth."

Philosophical Commentary

This opening verse sets the tone for the entire Upanishad. It eliminates the division between the sacred and the secular. Because the Divine envelops everything, no object or being is profane. Tena tyaktena bhuñjīthā instructs us to experience life not through greedy appropriation or possessiveness, but through joyful detachment—recognizing that nothing truly belongs to the ego.

2 Mantra 2: The Path of Action & Duty

Verse 2 of 18

कुर्वन्नेवेह कर्माणि जिजीविषेच्छतं समाः |

एवं त्वयि नान्यथेतोऽस्ति न कर्म लिप्यते नरे || २ ||

kurvanneveha karmāṇi jijīviṣecchataṃ samāḥ |
evaṃ tvayi nānyatheto'sti na karma lipyate nare || 2 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

Kurvan eva iha: Performing actions indeed here
karmāṇi: Duties / works
jijīviṣet: One should wish to live
śatam samāḥ: A hundred years
evam tvayi: Thus it is for you
na anyathā itaḥ asti: There is no other way than this
na karma lipyate nare: Action does not cling to man

English Translation

"Performing indeed works here [in this world], one should wish to live a hundred years. Thus it is for you, not otherwise; action does not cling to man."

Philosophical Commentary

This verse directly addresses the dilemma of whether a spiritual seeker should abandon worldly life. The Upanishad emphatically states that one should desire to live a full life span performing righteous duties (Karma). When actions are performed without egoistic attachment and with the awareness of divine pervasiveness (Mantra 1), the binding karma (Karma Lipyate) does not stain the individual.

3 Mantra 3: The Peril of Spiritual Blindness

Verse 3 of 18

असुर्या नाम ते लोका अन्धेन तमसाऽऽवृताः |

ताँस्ते प्रेत्याभिगच्छन्ति ये के चात्महनो जनाः || ३ ||

asuryā nāma te lokā andhena tamasā'vṛtāḥ |
tāṃste pretyābhigacchanti ye ke cātmahano janāḥ || 3 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

Asuryāḥ nāma: Demoniac / sunless
te lokāḥ: Those worlds
andhena tamasā āvṛtāḥ: Covered by blinding darkness
tān te pretya: To those worlds they, after death, go
abhigacchanti: Go / reach
ye ke ca: Whosoever
ātmahano janāḥ: Those people who are killers of the Self (ignorant of true nature)

English Translation

"Sunless [demoniac] are those worlds, covered by blinding darkness. To those worlds go after death all those people who are slayers of the Self."

Philosophical Commentary

Who are the ātmahanas (slayers of the Self)? They are not murderers of physical bodies, but those who live in complete ignorance of their true spiritual nature—identifying solely with the transient body and ego. Ignoring the divine reality described in Mantra 1 leads to psychological bondage and spiritual darkness, symbolized here as sunless demoniac worlds.

Practice Quiz: Mantras 1–3
