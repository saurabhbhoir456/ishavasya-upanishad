Module 3 • The Harmony of Opposites

Mantras 9 to 14 Breakdown

Reconciling empirical knowledge (Avidya) and spiritual wisdom (Vidya), along with the balance between the manifest universe and unmanifest reality.

Mantras & Detailed Breakdown

Study the Sanskrit verses, transliteration, and word-by-word meanings regarding Vidya and Avidya.

Mantra 9 Verse 9 of 18

अन्धं तमः प्रविशन्ति येऽविद्यामुपासते |
ततो भूय इव ते तमो य उ विद्यायां रताः || ९ ||

Andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye'vidyāmupāsate |
Tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u vidyāyāṃ ratāḥ || 9 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya)

andham tamaḥ: blind darkness
praviśanti: enter
ye: those who
avidyām upāsate: devote themselves to or pursue avidyā
tataḥ: than that
bhūyaḥ iva: as if, even greater
te tamaḥ: they (enter) darkness
ye u vidyāyām ratāḥ: those who are absorbed in vidyā

English Translation

“Into blind darkness enter those who pursue avidyā; into even greater darkness, as it were, enter those who delight in vidyā.” The precise senses of avidyā and vidyā are interpreted differently in Vedāntic traditions.

Philosophical Commentary

The verse warns against one-sided pursuit. Read alongside the next two mantras, it cautions that either pole—whose meanings vary by interpretive tradition—cannot by itself provide the complete way through human limitation. Its teaching prepares for the integration described in Mantra 11.

Mantras 10 & 11 Verses 10–11 of 18

अन्यदेवाहुर्विद्ययाऽन्यदाहुरविद्यया ।
इति शुश्रुम धीराणां ये नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे ॥ १० ॥

Anyad evāhur vidyayānyad āhur avidyayā |
Iti śuśruma dhīrāṇāṃ ye nas tad vicacakṣire || 10 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 10

anyat eva āhuḥ: they say it is indeed one thing
vidyayā: through vidyā
anyat āhuḥ: they say it is another thing
avidyayā: through avidyā
iti śuśruma: thus have we heard
dhīrāṇām: from the wise
ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire: who explained that to us

English Translation — Mantra 10

“They say that one result comes through vidyā and another through avidyā. Thus have we heard from the wise who explained it to us.”

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 10

This verse reports a teaching received from earlier sages: vidyā and avidyā are not interchangeable, and each has a distinct result. It serves as a bridge to Mantra 11, which considers knowing both together. The exact referents of these terms depend on the commentary tradition.

विद्यां चाविद्यां च यस्तद्वेदोभयं सह |
अविद्यया मृत्युं तीर्त्वा विद्ययामृतमश्नुते || ११ ||

Vidyāṃ cāvidyāṃ ca yastadvedobhayaṃ saha |
Avidyayā mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā vidyayāmṛtamaśnute || 11 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 11

yaḥ: whoever
vidyām ca avidyām ca: both vidyā and avidyā
tat veda: knows that
ubhayam saha: both together
avidyayā: through avidyā
mṛtyum tīrtvā: crossing over death
vidyayā: through vidyā
amṛtam aśnute: attains immortality

English Translation — Mantra 11

“Whoever knows vidyā and avidyā together crosses beyond death through avidyā and attains immortality through vidyā.”

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 11

The verse presents a paired discipline: each kind of knowledge has its own role and result, and the seeker is instructed to understand both. Traditional commentators differ on whether avidyā here refers to ritual action or another form of knowledge, and on the precise sense of crossing death and attaining immortality. The central emphasis is that neither term should be reduced to the other.

Mantras 12–14 Verses 12–14 of 18

अन्धं तमः प्रविशन्ति येऽसम्भूतिमुपासते ।
ततो भूय इव ते तमो य उ सम्भूत्या रताः ॥ १२ ॥

Andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'sambhūtim upāsate |
Tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u sambhūtyāṃ ratāḥ || 12 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 12

andham tamaḥ: blind darkness
praviśanti: enter
ye: those who
asambhūtim upāsate: attend to or worship the unmanifest / non-becoming
tataḥ: than that
bhūyaḥ iva: as if, even greater
te tamaḥ: they (enter) darkness
ye u sambhūtyām ratāḥ: those who are devoted to arising / becoming

English Translation — Mantra 12

“Into blind darkness enter those who worship asambhūti; into even greater darkness, as it were, enter those who delight in sambhūti.” The terms are rendered differently in traditional commentaries.

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 12

This parallel to Mantra 9 cautions against fixation on either pole of manifestation and non-manifestation. Because sambhūti and asambhūti have differing traditional interpretations, the verse is best read with Mantra 13 and the paired teaching in Mantra 14 rather than as a rejection of one side in isolation.

अन्यदेवाहुः सम्भवादन्यदाहुरसम्भवात् ।
इति शुश्रुम धीराणां ये नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे ॥ १३ ॥

Anyad evāhuḥ sambhavād anyad āhur asambhavāt |
Iti śuśruma dhīrāṇāṃ ye nas tad vicacakṣire || 13 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 13

anyat eva āhuḥ: they say it is indeed one thing
sambhavāt: from sambhava / becoming
anyat āhuḥ: they say it is another thing
asambhavāt: from asambhava / non-becoming
iti śuśruma: thus have we heard
dhīrāṇām: from the wise
ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire: who explained that to us

English Translation — Mantra 13

“They say that one result comes from sambhava and another from asambhava. Thus have we heard from the wise who explained it to us.”

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 13

As Mantra 10 does for vidyā and avidyā, this verse distinguishes the results associated with becoming and non-becoming. It establishes the need to discern both teachings before the next mantra explains their combined pursuit. Translators differ on the exact philosophical referents of these terms.

संभूतिं च विनाशं च यस्तद्वेदोभयं सह |
विनाशेन मृत्युं तीर्त्वा संभूत्यामृतमश्नुते || १४ ||

Saṃbhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca yastadvedobhayaṃ saha |
Vināśena mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā saṃbhūtyāmṛtamaśnute || 14 ||

Word-by-Word Meaning (Anvaya) — Mantra 14

yaḥ: whoever
sambhūtim ca vināśam ca: both sambhūti and vināśa
tat veda: knows that
ubhayam saha: both together
vināśena: through vināśa / the perishable
mṛtyum tīrtvā: crossing over death
sambhūtyā: through sambhūti / becoming
amṛtam aśnute: attains immortality

English Translation — Mantra 14

“Whoever knows sambhūti and vināśa together crosses beyond death through vināśa and attains immortality through sambhūti.” The Sanskrit terms have more than one traditional interpretation.

Philosophical Commentary — Mantra 14

The verse completes the paired teaching of Mantras 12–13: discernment includes both the changing and its counterpart, rather than attachment to only one. Commentarial traditions understand sambhūti and vināśa/asambhūti in different ways; this translation preserves the Sanskrit terms to avoid imposing a single interpretation.

Sanskrit text checked against SanskritDocuments.org: Īśopaniṣat. Translations and interpretations are provided here for study.

Core Philosophical Takeaway

Mantras 9–14 distinguish vidyā and avidyā, then sambhūti and asambhūti (or vināśa)—terms interpreted differently across Vedāntic traditions. The verses distinguish the results associated with each and consider knowing paired principles together. Their teaching is best read with attention to the commentary tradition rather than reducing each term to one fixed English equivalent.
